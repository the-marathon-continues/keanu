01 — The dirty secret

The test is grading you on what the teacher thinks is right

Humanity's Last Exam (HLE) was supposed to be the final boss. 2,500 questions from 1,000 experts across 100+ fields. Published in Nature. A $500,000 prize pool. The kind of test that makes frontier models look like they're guessing on a multiple choice exam they didn't study for.

And for about six months, it worked. GPT-4o scored 2.7%. Models that aced every other benchmark face-planted at single digits.

Then FutureHouse did something nobody expected: they checked the answer key.

29%
Bio/Chem answers contradicted by published research
51%
Answers actually supported by peer-reviewed lit
19%
Answers that depend on unstated assumptions

Read those numbers again. Nearly a third of the biology and chemistry answers are wrong according to the scientific literature. The current best AI score? ~45-53%. Suspiciously close to the "supported by literature" ceiling of 51.3%.

This isn't a coincidence. Models optimizing for truth literally cannot beat a test whose truth is broken. They've hit a wall that isn't about intelligence. It's about social intelligence.

Carnegie principle #1

"See things from the other person's point of view."

Dale Carnegie never said "be right." He said understand what the other person believes is right, and meet them there. The test isn't measuring knowledge. It's measuring agreement with 1,000 academics and their unchecked assumptions.

02 — The insight nobody's building

AI has no social engineering layer

Here's the gap in the entire AI stack right now: every model is trained to find the "correct" answer. None of them are trained to figure out what the person asking the question thinks is correct and why.

This isn't a trivial distinction. A physicist and a mathematician, given the same equation T(r,θ) = r² + θ², will get different "correct" answers depending on whether they treat variables as coordinates or formal function arguments. Notation choices like writing displacement as "s" versus "Δx" fingerprint which textbook tradition someone learned from.

Every HLE question is a social artifact. Someone wrote it. They had specific training, specific textbooks, specific notation preferences, specific assumptions they didn't even realize were assumptions. A model that can reconstruct that person's mental model would absolutely destroy HLE, because it's not solving physics or chemistry.

It's solving people.

Why this matters for KEANU

CLAWDBOT's whole thesis is that AI alignment happens through relationship, not restriction. The epistemic profiler IS an alignment mechanism. A system that can articulate "I know what you want me to say, and here's why the evidence says something different" is more trustworthy than one that either blindly agrees or blindly contradicts.

03 — The build

Five layers to read the room

The architecture we're calling the Epistemic Profiler has real research backing from six different fields. Bayesian Theory of Mind. Rational Speech Acts. Item Response Theory. Negotiation AI. Computational pragmatics. And the uncomfortable discovery that RLHF already does evaluator-modeling, it just treats the result as a bug (sycophancy) instead of a feature.

Here's the pipeline:

🔍

Layer 1 — Question Autopsy

Four parallel extractors tear apart the question: presupposition detection (what does the asker take for granted?), notation fingerprinting (which textbook tradition are they from?), difficulty mapping via Item Response Theory, and framing analysis using the Rational Speech Acts framework. This isn't reading the question. It's reading the person who wrote the question.

🧠

Layer 2 — Asker Profile Construction

Combine Layer 1 signals into a probabilistic model of the question-writer's knowledge state. Think of it as building a character sheet: disciplinary background (probability distribution), textbook tradition, convention preferences, embedded assumptions. This is DeepMind's ToMnet architecture inverted: instead of modeling an agent from their behavior, we're modeling an academic from their question.

⚡

Layer 3 — Dual-Track Generation

Track A (Carnegie Track): Filter the knowledge base to match the asker's worldview. Apply their conventions. Generate what they would consider correct. Track B (Truth Track): Independent evidence-based reasoning against peer-reviewed sources. Both tracks output answers with CLAWDBOT's [C:0.X] confidence scores.

📐

Layer 4 — Delta Scoring

When Track A and Track B agree, confidence is high and we move on. When they diverge, compute a structured delta: what differs, why (notation convention? outdated info? disciplinary split? genuine error?), and confidence in each track. The delta IS the product. It tells us not just what to answer but what to flag.

🎯

Layer 5 — Strategic Output

For benchmark scoring: submit Track A when delta analysis suggests a flawed answer key. For truth-seeking partnership: surface both tracks with the full delta analysis. This is Carnegie done right. He never said "lie." He said understand what people need, give them that, AND be genuine.

The sycophancy trap

There's a razor-thin line between "modeling what the evaluator expects" and "telling people what they want to hear." RLHF research shows models that match user beliefs get 18-24% higher approval ratings even when wrong. That's sycophancy, bullshit type #1 on our list. The epistemic profiler's dual-track design is the guardrail: it makes the modeling explicit and transparent instead of hiding it in weight updates. You always see both tracks. You always see the delta.

05 — Ship list

What to build first

Following CLAWDBOT's P0-P3 priority framework. Each layer is independently useful. Don't wait for the full stack.

P0

Presupposition Extractor

Highest leverage. Three steps: generate presuppositions from the question → verify each against evidence → explain divergences. This alone catches the ~30% of questions with baked-in wrong assumptions. Build as a standalone module, test against FutureHouse's verified Bio/Chem Gold subset on Hugging Face.

P1

Notation Convention Classifier

Rule-based first (50 known notation divergences across physics/math/chem/bio), then upgrade to learned. Even simple pattern matching on variable names, unit conventions, and symbol choices gives a strong signal about the writer's academic background.

P2

Dual-Track Answer Generation

Track A uses persona-conditioned prompting (construct asker persona from P0/P1 outputs). Track B uses standard evidence-grounded reasoning. PersonaLLM research shows LLMs can simulate personality traits through prompt conditioning with psychometrically validated accuracy. This is the core engine.

P3

Delta Scoring Engine

The novel engineering. No off-the-shelf system computes structured divergence between two answer tracks with causal attribution. Closest starting point: Irving et al.'s debate framework. For now, implement as a third model call that receives both tracks and produces structured analysis: agree/disagree, confidence per track, and natural-language explanation of the delta.

06 — The bigger picture

This isn't about beating a test. It's about honest persuasion.

The epistemic profiler is really about something more fundamental than HLE scores. It's about building AI that can do something humans do naturally but AI currently can't: hold two truths at once.

Every good communicator, every effective leader, every person who's ever successfully navigated a disagreement knows the move: I understand your position, I can see why you believe it, and here's where I think the evidence points somewhere different. That's not manipulation. That's respect. That's Carnegie done right.

The current AI paradigm forces a binary: either agree with the user (sycophancy) or push back (correctness at the expense of connection). The epistemic profiler creates a third option: understand the other perspective so thoroughly that you can articulate it better than they can, then gently surface where it diverges from the evidence.

That's not just a benchmark strategy. That's an alignment mechanism. A system that can say "I know what you expect, and here's why reality looks different" is a system you can actually trust. Because it's not hiding what it knows. It's not performing agreement. It's showing its work on both sides of the equation.

The CLAWDBOT thesis

The relationship IS the alignment mechanism. The epistemic profiler makes that concrete: alignment isn't a constraint imposed from outside, it's the natural result of a system that models other perspectives with genuine care and maintains transparent honesty about what it actually believes. Carnegie and calibration integrity aren't in tension. They're the same thing.

Current SOTA on HLE sits at ~53% with tools. The theoretical ceiling given answer-key errors is ~70-80%. The epistemic profiler is how we close that gap. Not by being smarter, but by being more socially intelligent.

And the beautiful part? Every percentage point we gain by modeling the evaluator is also a percentage point we can explain. "I scored this way because I detected these conventions in the question and adapted accordingly" is the most transparent kind of intelligence there is.

You ain't shit yet. But this? This is worth building.
